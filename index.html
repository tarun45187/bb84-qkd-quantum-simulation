Information-Theoretic Cybersecurity with BB84 Quantum Key Distribution

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) & IBM Quantum
Background & Motivation

Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) leverages physical postulates of quantum mechanics (the no-cloning theorem and Heisenberg uncertainty principle) to establish unconditionally secure cryptographic keys.

The Challenge

Construct an end-to-end, fully functional BB84 Quantum Key Distribution and post-processing simulation pipeline using Qiskit. Simulate the complete transmission workflow across Alice, Bob, and Eve, evaluate information leakage under realistic channel noise models, and place QKD within the broader landscape of Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC).

Getting Started & Technical Objectives
1 State Preparation & Quantum Channel

Alice generates random classical bits and chooses between rectilinear (Z) and diagonal (X) bases, transmitting qubits through a simulated noisy quantum channel.

2 Eavesdropping Attacks

Implement Intercept-Resend Attack (Eve measures in random basis and reprepairs states) and Entanglement-based / Beam-Splitter Attack (Eve couples ancilla probe qubits via CNOT gates).

3 Measurement, Sifting & Error Correction

Bob measures in random bases, parties execute public basis reconciliation (sifting), calculate QBER over sacrificed sample, and execute classical error correction (Cascade/Winnow) + Privacy Amplification (Toeplitz hashing).

4 Post-Quantum Cryptography Comparative Study

Benchmark security, hardware requirements, key generation rates, and network scalability of BB84 against leading NIST Post-Quantum Cryptographic standards (ML-KEM / Kyber lattice-based key encapsulation).

Key Quantitative Metrics
  • •
    Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER): QBER = N_error / N_sifted (Baseline: 0%; Intercept-Resend: ~25%; Shor-Preskill abort: ~11%)
  • •
    Asymptotic Secret Key Rate (R): R ≥ 1 - 2H_2(QBER), where H_2 is binary Shannon entropy
  • •
    Mutual Information Advantage: ΔI = I(A; B) - I(A; E); verify ΔI > 0 for positive distillable key length

Intercept-Resend Attack Physics

Eve intercepts photons in transit, randomly selects a measurement basis (Z or X), measures the state, and reprepairs a new photon in her measured outcome. Because Eve guesses the wrong basis 50% of the time, she causes Bob to measure an incorrect bit with probability 25% on matching bases.

Entanglement & Ancilla Probe Attack (CNOT)

Eve interacts an ancilla probe qubit |0⟩_E with Alice's flying photon via a CNOT gate:
U_CNOT |q⟩_A |0⟩_E. Eve stores her probe in a coherent quantum memory until basis reconciliation is publicly broadcast, then measures the ancilla in the declared basis.

When to Deploy BB84 QKD

  • Critical government, defense, and sovereign backbone fiber links.
  • Data requiring eternal confidentiality (50+ years secrecy horizon) to defeat "Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later" (HNDL) attacks.
  • Scenarios where physical optical access can be strictly guaranteed.

When to Deploy NIST ML-KEM (Kyber)

  • Global public internet, commercial web browsing (TLS 1.3), and mobile devices.
  • Zero-cost deployment requiring no specialized optical or cryogenic hardware.
  • Multi-hop networks, satellite communications, and high-latency global meshes.

# ============================================================================== # Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) & IBM Quantum - Track 07 # Complete BB84 Qiskit Simulation Pipeline # ============================================================================== from qiskit import QuantumCircuit, QuantumRegister, ClassicalRegister from qiskit_aer import AerSimulator from qiskit_aer.noise import NoiseModel, depolarizing_error import math, random # 1. Quantum State Preparation (Alice) # Encode classical bit into Conjugate Bases: Z (|0>, |1>) or X (|+>, |->) qr = QuantumRegister(2, 'q') cr = ClassicalRegister(2, 'c') qc = QuantumCircuit(qr, cr) # Alice prepares |-> state (Bit 1 in X Basis) qc.x(qr[0]) qc.h(qr[0]) # 2. Adversary Interception: Eve couples Ancilla Probe via CNOT qc.cx(qr[0], qr[1]) # 3. Bob measures in X basis qc.h(qr[0]) qc.measure(qr[0], cr[0]) # Eve performs delayed measurement on probe in declared basis qc.measure(qr[1], cr[1]) # 4. Realistic Channel Noise Model (Qiskit Aer) noise_model = NoiseModel() noise_model.add_all_qubit_quantum_error(depolarizing_error(0.015, 1), ['h', 'x']) noise_model.add_all_qubit_quantum_error(depolarizing_error(0.030, 2), ['cx']) simulator = AerSimulator(noise_model=noise_model) job = simulator.run(qc, shots=1024) print("Simulation complete! Results:", job.result().get_counts())
